# app | Using the app

The last section explains the app itself: each tab and screen, what a tapped word shows, the short forms used under words, how Review decides what to show you, and how listening, offline use, backups and updates work.

## word-sheet | What a tapped word shows
lang: both
aka: word details, word sheet, tapped word, In this verse, Dictionary form, Dictionary meaning, How common, Word parts, What do these words mean?
see: gloss, dictionary-form, grammar-labels, strongs, how-common, prefixes
short: Tap any Greek or Hebrew word in the Reader to see the word as it stands, its English in this verse, its [[dictionary-form]] and meaning, its grammar and how common it is.

The details open in a panel from the bottom of the screen. From the top:

- the word as written, with its [[transliteration]] and a Listen button;
- **In this verse**: the English for this word here, from STEPBible's word-by-word translation ([[gloss]]);
- **Word parts** (Hebrew): each [[prefixes|prefix]], the main word and any [[pronoun-suffix|ending]], with their meanings;
- **Dictionary form**: the form you would look up ([[dictionary-form]]), with its [[strongs|Strong's number]];
- **Dictionary meaning**: the word's usual meaning;
- **Grammar**: its grammar label, a plain description, and the link “What do these words mean?”, which explains each part of the label;
- **How common**: how often the dictionary form appears ([[how-common]]).

Below, the verse's English shows for context, and Previous and Next move along the verse. Tapping the dictionary form opens the full entry in the [[words-tab|Words tab]]. Today's verse and a lesson's Read step show a shorter version of the same details.

## listening-levels | Listening practice: Easy, Medium and Hard
lang: both
aka: listening practice, listen level, Easy, Medium, Hard, hear the verse, Listen buttons, voices
see: erasmian, restored-koine, hebrew-pronunciation, word-sheet
short: A setting that decides how verses can be heard. Easy: tap any word to hear it. Medium: that, plus “Listen to the verse”. Hard: whole verses only.

Settings › Listening practice applies to Today's verse, a lesson's Read step, the Reader, the [[septuagint]] and the [[letters-tab|Letters tab]]'s verses:

- **Easy**: tapping a word says it at once, and shows its meaning; there is no whole-verse button, so you build up a verse word by word;
- **Medium** (the starting setting): tapping a word says it, and “Listen to the verse” plays the whole verse;
- **Hard**: only whole verses can be heard; tapping a word shows its meaning silently, to train your ear on connected speech.

These levels are separate from the Again, Hard, Good and Easy buttons in [[review-scheduling|Review]]. The dictionary, Review and this guide keep their Listen buttons at every level. The voices are your phone's own Greek and Hebrew voices. If nothing plays, see [[no-sound]].

## review-scheduling | How Review decides what to show
lang: both
aka: review, flashcards, spaced repetition, FSRS, Again, Hard, Good, Easy, due, new words, cards, review schedule
see: how-common, listening-levels
short: Review shows each word just before you are likely to forget it. Your answer (Again, Hard, Good or Easy) decides when the card comes back.

Review uses a scheduling method called FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler), worked out from millions of real reviews and freely shared. Each time you see a card and say how well you knew it, it estimates how long you will remember the word, and brings the card back a little before then. Words you find easy come back after longer and longer gaps; words you miss come back soon, sometimes in the same session.

- **Again**: you didn't know it. It comes back within minutes.
- **Hard**: you knew it with difficulty. It comes back sooner than usual.
- **Good**: you knew it. The usual gap.
- **Easy**: you knew it at once. A longer gap.

These buttons are separate from the Easy, Medium and Hard [[listening-levels|listening levels]].

New words come first from the lessons you have finished, then from the word lists you switch on in Review (the most common words first: see [[how-common]]), a few each day. You can change how many in Review's settings. The day's reviews count towards your [[study-day|study days]].

## study-day | Study days and streaks
lang: both
aka: streak, study day, days in a row, 4am, calendar, progress
see: review-scheduling
short: A day of study counts if you review, work on a lesson, or read a chapter for half a minute. The study day starts at four in the morning, so late-night study counts for the day before.

Your streak is the number of days in a row with some study, up to today (or up to yesterday, until you study today). The [[progress-screen|Progress screen]] shows it, with a calendar of the last twelve weeks.

Because many people study late in the evening, a new study day starts at four in the morning rather than at midnight. Review uses the same day: cards due “today” are due before the next four o'clock. The change of the clocks in spring and autumn doesn't move it.

## septuagint-lookup | Looking up Septuagint words
lang: grc
aka: Septuagint word, LXX lookup, guess, Septuagint dictionary, untagged
see: septuagint, dictionary-form
short: The [[septuagint]]'s words are not tagged, so when you tap one the app looks for its spelling among the forms it knows. If it can't find the word exactly, it shows its best guess and says so.

Every New Testament word in the app is tagged with its [[dictionary-form]]. The Septuagint text the app uses is not. So the app looks the tapped word up by its spelling: first among the forms of the New Testament and the dictionary's headwords, allowing for the Septuagint's spelling habits, then by looking for a dictionary word with the same stem and a usual ending. A guess is labelled as a guess. Names are only matched with names.

Most tapped words are found exactly; a few get a guess or nothing. The dictionary search is always offered as well.

## reader-layouts | Reader layouts and translations
lang: both
aka: word by word, running text, side by side, layout, display options, parallel, interlinear
see: translations, septuagint, word-sheet, abbreviations
short: The Reader shows a chapter word by word (each word with its English under it), as running text (like a printed page), or side by side with translations.

Display options (the button at the top of the Reader) choose the layout and what shows with it:

- **Word by word**: each Greek or Hebrew word with its English, [[transliteration|pronunciation]] and, if you choose, its grammar in [[abbreviations|short form]] under it. The BSB, ESV or [[septuagint]] can show under each verse;
- **Running text**: the original as a page of paragraphs, with nothing under the words; tap a word for its details;
- **Side by side**: each verse in columns with the translations you choose (one under another on a phone).

Whatever the layout, tapping a word opens its [[word-sheet|details]].

## offline-saving | Using the app offline
lang: both
aka: offline, save it all, no connection, download, aeroplane mode, flight mode
see: backup
short: The app keeps working without a connection. It saves what you use, and Settings › Offline › “Save it all” saves the whole Bible and dictionary in one go.

Once the app is on your home screen, its screens, lessons, word lists and this guide work offline straight away. Bible books, translations and dictionary entries are saved as you open them, and a few seconds after you open the app it saves the books the lessons and [[letters-tab|Letters tab]] need. To have everything for a trip, use Settings › Offline › “Save it all for offline use” while you are on Wi-Fi. The ESV can only be partly saved, because its publisher limits how much may be kept.

## backup | Backups
lang: both
aka: backup, save progress, restore, export, second device, new phone
see: offline-saving, study-day
short: Your progress is kept on your phone. A backup is a small file you can save or send yourself, and restore on another device or after a reset.

The app doesn't have accounts, so everything you do (lessons, review cards, streak, settings) stays on the device. Settings (“Save backup”) and the [[progress-screen|Progress screen]] (“Save a backup”) make a file. On an iPhone or iPad it opens the share sheet, so you can save it to Files or send it to yourself. To move to another device, open the app there and restore the file in Settings. Today reminds you to save a backup now and then once you have some progress to lose.

## lesson-steps | The steps of a lesson
lang: both
aka: lesson, lessons, Hear and see, Notice, Practise, Read, Review step, five steps
see: review-scheduling, word-sheet
short: Every lesson has five steps: Hear and see, Notice, Practise, Read (a real verse), and Review (its words go into your Review cards).

- **Hear and see**: meet the new sounds or words in a simple scene, with videos to watch alongside;
- **Notice**: short explanations with tables and examples you can hear;
- **Practise**: quick exercises. A question you miss comes back once at the end;
- **Read**: a real verse from the Bible, with the words you know underlined;
- **Review**: the lesson's words, which then join your Review cards.

You can leave a lesson at any point and pick up where you left off. The explanations in each lesson link to the entries in this guide that go further.

## today-screen | The Today screen
lang: both
aka: Today, home, daily plan, your plan for today, today's verse, verse of the day, streak
see: study-day, review-scheduling, listening-levels, word-sheet, backup
short: The first tab. It shows your streak, a verse to read in the language you are learning, and a plan for the day: the next lesson, your reviews, a video and any reminder.

At the top, once you have started, your streak of [[study-day|study days]] links to the [[progress-screen|Progress screen]].

Next comes today's verse, in Greek or Hebrew, whichever you are learning (switch with the buttons above it). A new verse comes each day, chosen so that no verse returns until all have had their turn, and every device shows the same one on the same day. Tap a word for its meaning, its grammar and the link “What do these words mean?”; how verses are heard follows your [[listening-levels|listening practice]] setting.

Below the verse, “Your plan for today” lists the next lesson on your path and the number of Review cards due. It also suggests a video to go with your next lesson, reminds you to save a [[backup]] when one is due, and shows how far you have come.

## words-tab | The Words tab: searching the dictionary
lang: both
aka: Words, dictionary, search, look up, lexicon, find a word, Words tab
see: dictionary-form, strongs, how-common, transliteration, gloss
short: The Words tab is the dictionary. Search in English, in Greek or Hebrew (with or without accents and vowels), by a word as it stands in a verse, by its sound, or by Strong's number.

Type into the search box and matching entries appear as you type. You can search by:

- English: “shepherd” finds ποιμήν and רָעָה;
- Greek or Hebrew, with or without accents and vowel marks;
- a word as it stands in a verse: ἠγάπησεν finds ἀγαπάω, labelled “Dictionary form of your word”;
- its sound in English letters: kyrios, bereshit, bara elohim;
- [[strongs|Strong's number]]: G3056 or H430.

The buttons above the results limit the search to Greek or Hebrew. Each entry shows the [[dictionary-form]], its meanings, its forms in the Bible and every verse where it appears, each a link into the Reader. Grammar words you search for, such as “aorist”, also show a link to their entry in this guide.

## progress-screen | The Progress screen
lang: both
aka: Progress, calendar, statistics, how am I doing, words known, share of the text
see: study-day, review-scheduling, how-common, backup
short: A summary of your study: your streak, a calendar of the last twelve weeks, lessons finished, words in Review by how well you know them, and the cards due in the week ahead.

Open it from Today's streak line or the Learn tab (on an iPad, from the side rail). The calendar shades each day by how much you did; tap a day to see what it was.

For each language it shows lessons finished, letters known, and your Review words in three groups: known well, getting there, and still new. “Share of the text” adds up how often the words you know well appear, as a share of all the words in the Greek or Hebrew Bible: because a few words are very common ([[how-common]]), it grows quickly at first. At the bottom are the cards due on each of the next seven days, and the [[backup]] button.

## letters-tab | The Letters tab
lang: both
aka: Letters, alphabet guide, alphabet and sounds, letter cards, read aloud
see: greek-alphabet, hebrew-alphabet, vowel-points, erasmian, restored-koine, hebrew-pronunciation
short: Every Greek and Hebrew letter with its name, its sound and examples you can hear, followed by the vowels, marks and signs, and verses to read aloud.

The Letters tab opens on the alphabet you looked at last; the buttons at the top switch between Greek and Hebrew. Tap a letter for its own page, with its forms, its sound, a tip and an example word, and Previous and Next to move along the alphabet. The Greek letters show both [[erasmian|Erasmian]] and [[restored-koine|Restored Koine]] sounds, marked with the one the app is using.

Below the letters come the vowels, the marks and the signs printed in the text, then a few verses to read aloud word by word. To learn the letters by heart, switch on the letter cards in Review's settings.

## no-sound | No sound from a Listen button
lang: both
aka: no sound, voices, silent, Listen doesn't work, add a voice, ringer switch, mute, speech
see: listening-levels, erasmian, hebrew-pronunciation
short: The app speaks with your phone's own Greek and Hebrew voices. If nothing plays, check the volume and, on an iPhone, the ringer switch, then make sure a Greek or Hebrew voice is installed.

Things to try, in order:

- turn the volume up;
- on an iPhone, check the ringer (silent) switch on the side: when it is set to silent, the voices are silent too;
- add the voice. On an iPhone or iPad: Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › Voices, then Greek or Hebrew, and download one. On Android: Settings, search for “Text-to-speech”, open the speech engine's settings and install the Greek or Hebrew voice data;
- run Settings › Help › “Check this device”, which tries each voice and asks whether you heard it.

The voices are modern Greek and Israeli Hebrew ones. The app respells each word for them so that they come out as close as they can to the pronunciation you have chosen (see [[erasmian]], [[restored-koine]] and [[hebrew-pronunciation]]).

## settings-overview | Settings at a glance
lang: both
aka: Settings, options, preferences, text size, dark mode, appearance, pronunciation setting
see: listening-levels, erasmian, restored-koine, translations, offline-saving, backup
short: Settings (the gear at the top of each screen, or on the iPad's side rail) holds text size, appearance, pronunciation, listening, the ESV key, offline saving, [[backup|backups]] and help.

- **About**: where the texts and dictionaries come from, and their licences.

The Reader's own display options (the button at the top of the Reader) choose its layout and what shows under the words: see [[reader-layouts]].

## updating | Getting the latest version
lang: both
aka: update, new version, latest version, refresh, reload, close and reopen
see: offline-saving
short: The app updates itself. To see a new version straight away, close the app completely and open it again, twice: the first time fetches the update, the second shows it.

Because the app works offline, it keeps a copy of itself on your device. Each time you open it with a connection, it checks for a newer copy and fetches it in the background, and the next time you open it you see the new version. So after an update is announced, close the app fully (swipe it away from the app switcher) and reopen it twice. Your progress, settings and saved books are kept.
