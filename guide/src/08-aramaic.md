# aramaic | Biblical Aramaic

A few chapters of the Old Testament are in Aramaic, Hebrew's close relative. Its letters, vowels and much of its vocabulary are the same, but its verb stems have different names and its word for “the” is an ending. This section explains the labels you will meet there.

## aramaic-stems | Aramaic verb stems
lang: arc
aka: Aramaic stems, Peal, Pael, Haphel, Aramaic verbs
see: stems, aramaic-language, peal, pael, haphel, hitpeel
find: arc (^|/)V n=2
short: Aramaic verbs use [[stems]] like Hebrew ones, under different names: [[peal]] is the simple stem (like [[qal]]), [[pael]] doubles the middle letter (like [[piel]]), and [[haphel|Haphel]] is causative (like [[hiphil]]).

Aramaic builds its verbs the same way as Hebrew: a [[root]] fitted into a pattern called a stem. The patterns match closely, but grammars use Aramaic names for them, made from the Aramaic root פעל, “to do”, as Hebrew names come from פעל too.

Table: Aramaic stems and their Hebrew partners
| Aramaic stem | like Hebrew | usual kind of action |
| [[peal]] | [[qal]] | simple, active |
| [[peil]] | passive of the Qal | simple, passive |
| [[pael]] | [[piel]] | intensive or “making” |
| [[haphel|Haphel]] (also aphel, shaphel) | [[hiphil]] | causative |
| [[hitpeel|Hitpeel]] (and hitpaal) | [[niphal]] or [[hithpael]] | passive or reflexive |

Aramaic verb forms are the [[perfect-hebrew|perfect]] and [[imperfect-hebrew|imperfect]], as in Hebrew, but without the narrative “and” forms ([[wayyiqtol]] and [[weqatal]]). Stories in Aramaic usually use the perfect, or a [[participle]], for the past.

## peal | Peal
lang: arc
aka: Peal, peʿal, G stem in Aramaic
match: peal=the simple stem of Aramaic, like the Hebrew Qal
see: aramaic-stems, qal
find: arc (^|/)Vq n=2
short: The simple stem of Aramaic verbs, matching the Hebrew [[qal]]: אֲמַר, “he said”.

Most Aramaic verbs in Daniel and Ezra are Peal. It gives the root its plain meaning, active: אֲמַר, “he said”, עֲנוֹ, “they answered”. The common Aramaic phrase עָנֵה… וְאָמַר, “answering… and saying”, is Peal.

## peil | Peil
lang: arc
aka: Peil, peʿil, Peal passive
match: peil=the passive of the simple stem in Aramaic
see: peal, aramaic-stems, passive
find: arc (^|/)VQ n=2
short: The passive of the [[peal]], used a few times in Daniel and Ezra: “it was written”, “it was given”.

The Peil turns the simple stem [[passive]]: יְהִיבַת, “it was given”. It is rare, and a passive meaning is the clue.

## pael | Pael
lang: arc
aka: Pael, paʿel, D stem in Aramaic
match: pael=the stem with a doubled middle letter, like the Hebrew Piel
see: aramaic-stems, piel
find: arc (^|/)Vp n=2
short: The Aramaic stem with a doubled middle letter, matching the Hebrew [[piel]]: מְשַׁבַּח, “praising”.

Like the Piel, the Pael doubles the middle letter of the root with a [[dagesh]], and often has a “making” or stronger meaning. Some verbs simply use it as their usual stem: מְשַׁבַּח, “praising”, in [DAN 2:23|Daniel 2:23].

## haphel | Haphel, aphel and shaphel
lang: arc
aka: Haphel, Aphel, Shaphel, causative stem in Aramaic, H stem in Aramaic
match: haphel=the causative stem of Aramaic, like the Hebrew Hiphil; aphel=the causative stem of Aramaic, with א instead of ה; shaphel=a rare causative stem with שׁ; ishtaphel=a rare reflexive of the shaphel
see: aramaic-stems, hiphil
find: arc (^|/)V[hae] n=2
short: The causative stems of Aramaic: “to make someone do”. The Haphel starts with ה, the Aphel with א, and the rare Shaphel with שׁ.

These match the Hebrew [[hiphil]]: הַשְׁפִּיל, “he humbled”, Haphel. The Aphel is the same stem with א in place of the ה, a later spelling. The Shaphel starts with שׁ and is used by a few verbs, especially שֵׁיזִב, “he rescued”. The ishtaphel is a rare reflexive form of it.

Their passive is the [[hophal]], which STEPBible labels with the Hebrew name.

## hitpeel | Hitpeel and hitpaal
lang: arc
aka: Hitpeel, Hithpeel, Hitpaal, Hithpaal, hitpael, Aramaic passive, Aramaic reflexive
match: hitpeel=the passive or reflexive of the Peal; hitpaal=the passive or reflexive of the Pael; hitpael=the passive or reflexive of the Pael
see: aramaic-stems, hithpael, niphal
find: arc (^|/)V[iPMu] n=2
short: The Aramaic stems that start with הִתְ- and are passive or reflexive: the Hitpeel of the [[peal]], and the Hitpaal of the [[pael]].

Aramaic has no [[niphal]]. For “to be done”, it puts הִתְ- or אִתְ- in front of the simple or the doubled stem: תִּתְבְּנֵא, “it will be built”, in the letters in Ezra. STEPBible's labels spell these stems in more than one way (hitpeel, hitpaal, hitpael); all of them are passive or reflexive.

## emphatic-state | “The” in Aramaic: the definite ending
lang: arc
aka: emphatic state, determined state, definite, -a ending, Aramaic article, emph.
match: definite=with “the”: the ending -א
see: article, absolute-state, construct-state
find: arc ^N...d n=2
short: Aramaic says “the” with an ending, usually -א: מַלְכָּא, “the king”, from מֶלֶךְ, “a king”. STEPBible's labels call this form “definite”.

Hebrew puts “the” at the front of a word ([[hebrew-article]]). Aramaic puts it at the end: מֶלֶךְ is “a king”, מַלְכָּא is “the king”. Grammars call this the emphatic or determined state. Besides this form, Aramaic nouns have the [[absolute-state|absolute]] and [[construct-state|construct]] forms, just as Hebrew ones do.

The ending also shows in Aramaic words the New Testament keeps, such as Αββα, “Father”, from אַבָּא.

unattested: אַבָּא
